Interpretation of negative pre-calibration R² (EMS air temperature). For the EMS
air-temperature channel (n=36), the uncalibrated sensor–meter comparison yielded
R² = -4.28 with RMSE = 0.34 °C (95% CI from bootstrap in the summary table)
and mean bias ≈ -0.29 °C. A negative R² does not imply that absolute temperature
error is large; it follows directly from
R² = 1 − SS_res/SS_tot.

R² < 0 occurs whenever residual mean squared error exceeds the variance of the reference
series—i.e., when predicting the sample mean of the meter would outperform the raw sensor
readings. In this campaign the meter temperatures spanned only a narrow dynamic range
(≈ 32.9–33.6 °C; span = 0.70 °C), so SS_tot was
very small (0.7675), while a nearly constant systematic offset between sensor and
meter kept SS_res larger (4.0500). Hence R² became strongly negative even though
absolute error remained modest (RMSE ≈ 0.34 °C).

After a simple linear mapping of sensor onto the meter scale
(meter ≈ 0.208·sensor + 26.40), R² increased to 0.033 and RMSE to 0.14 °C.
The remaining limited explained variance reflects insufficient temperature excursion during
collection, not catastrophic sensing failure. We therefore report RMSE, bias, and
Bland–Altman limits as the primary agreement measures for this channel, and treat
pre-calibration R² as a diagnostic of offset + narrow range rather than as evidence of
unusable absolute accuracy. EMS air-temperature readings require offset/scale calibration
against a reference meter before use as absolute climate truth; future validation should
intentionally span a wider temperature range.
