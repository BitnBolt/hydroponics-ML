Data collection. Sensor validation used paired ground-truth measurements on the BitnBolt
hydroponic platform. For each observation, the IoT sensor reading and a simultaneous
reference meter reading were logged as a matched pair. Four channels were validated:
NFADS TDS (n=42), NFADS water temperature (n=142), EMS air temperature (n=36),
and LMS illuminance BH1750 vs TSL2591 (n=753). Incomplete or non-numeric pairs were excluded.

Train/test procedure. This sensor–meter study is a paired agreement analysis, not a
supervised learning experiment. Therefore no train/test split or cross-validation fold
partitioning was applied to these paired samples; every retained pair contributes to
agreement metrics between the deployed sensor and the reference instrument. (ML models
reported elsewhere in the manuscript use separate time-series cross-validation and are
distinct from this ground-truth validation.)

Error metrics and confidence intervals. With the meter as reference y and the sensor as
ŷ, we computed RMSE, MAE, R², and mean bias. Uncertainty on RMSE/MAE/R²/bias was
quantified by nonparametric bootstrap with B=500 resamples with replacement; the
reported 95% CI is the interval between the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the bootstrap
distribution (fixed random seed for reproducibility). Calibration used OLS
(sensor = a·reference + b) with slope/intercept intervals from the t-distribution.
Bland–Altman analysis reported bias and limits of agreement (bias ± 1.96 SD).

Accuracy percentages. For agreement reporting we primarily use RMSE/MAE/R²/bias with 95% CI.
An optional in-tolerance accuracy was defined as the percentage of pairs satisfying
|ŷ − y|/|y| ≤ 3%. For TDS this rate was 40.5%; for EMS air temperature 100.0%.
This percentage is a descriptive fraction of paired samples inside the tolerance band,
not a train/test classification score.
